# Clinician design-partner operations kit

**Fasolati · Internal working kit · Version 01 · September 13, 2026**

**Status: PROPOSED. Not launched. No outreach sent. All invite and follow-up copy below is UNSENT and requires user approval of recipient, channel and complete payload before use.**

## Purpose and non-negotiable scope

Run a proposed 5–8-clinician, product-usability design review. Each clinician would take part in one 30-minute individual walkthrough using only supplied fictional cases and public product-information pages.

The output is an issue log and prioritized product-information changes. It is not a clinical study, proof of diagnostic performance, product trial, safety/efficacy assessment, clinical validation or professional endorsement. This kit does not make a determination about regulatory or ethics-review requirements. If the scope changes, stop and obtain appropriate governance review before restarting.

Do not recruit patients, request patient records, accept specimens, administer a product, measure clinical outcomes, discuss identifiable real cases or ask reviewers to alter care. Reviewers do not receive patient-level recommendations.

## Recruitment criteria

### Include

- A currently practicing clinician in an applicable professional role, or a clinician whose active work includes clinical informatics and information review.
- Experience explaining observations or product information to professional colleagues or patients, described without individual cases.
- Willingness to think aloud about an unfamiliar interface and challenge wording rather than endorse it.
- Ability to complete the fictional-material walkthrough and agree to the no-patient-data boundary.
- A mix of digital comfort levels and practice contexts; do not select only technology enthusiasts or known supporters.

### Do not include in this round

- Patients, minors or participants seeking personal medical advice.
- Anyone proposing to supply clinical datasets, patient histories, screenshots, samples or real-world patient outcomes.
- Anyone expecting treatment, a clinical product trial, guaranteed access, an endorsement role or compensation not expressly agreed.
- Anyone whose participation cannot fit the approved scope or note-taking arrangements.

Record self-described role and relevant information-review experience. If credentials need verification, use the participant-supplied professional information and an approved process; do not guess contacts or collect unrelated background.

## Three core research questions

**RQ1 — Meaning:** Can the clinician distinguish a measured observation, an interpretation, a hypothesis and an unsupported claim?

**RQ2 — Inspectability:** Can the reader find the source, date, method and limitations, and identify the point at which information is insufficient?

**RQ3 — Priority:** Which usability or wording change would make the presentation clearer without implying a treatment recommendation?

Assess behavior before preference: note what participants find, miss or misread before asking what they would like. Do not interpret a positive reaction as clinical support. Retain dissenting or contradictory observations rather than forcing a consensus.

## Moderator script: 30 minutes

**Do not improvise a clinical recommendation.** Read neutral prompts. After a task, ask “What made you think that?” before explaining the intended design.

### 00:00–04:00 · Scope, permission and orientation

> Thank you for considering this design review. We are reviewing how information is presented, not testing your clinical knowledge and not validating a product. Everything in the case walkthrough is fictional. Please do not describe real patients or show clinical records.
>
> I would like to take manual notes about the interface and your interpretation. There is no audio or video recording. Before we begin, are you comfortable with the scope and the note-handling arrangements we sent? You can skip a question or stop the conversation.

Confirm permission; if not agreed, do not proceed. Ask for role and general information-review context without asking for clinical cases.

### 04:00–09:00 · First reading, without explanation

Show the approved Life Board introduction and Loria dossier heading.

> In your own words, what is this offering today? Which parts appear to be product information, a development concept or an evaluated clinical service?
>
> What on this page makes you say that?
>
> Before opening any extra detail, what would you expect the next button to do?

Record spontaneous interpretation, hesitations and phrases mistaken for promises. Do not correct misunderstandings yet unless they cross the safety boundary.

### 09:00–18:00 · Fictional-case walkthrough

Use the approved version of the guided Life Board at `/life-board/`. Walk through initial observation, follow-up, discordance and missing data. All four visits are entirely fictional; do not replace them with an actual patient case.

> This board describes a local change, a separately recorded host observation, disagreement between the channels and a later incomplete visit. No real person is represented. A colleague asks whether the display proves that an oral product improved systemic health.
>
> Please show where you would look before deciding what this screen actually supports. We are testing the presentation, not asking you to treat a patient.

Tasks:

1. Find the observation and identify what was actually measured versus interpreted.
2. Find date, method, source or missingness information; if a field is absent, say so rather than infer it.
3. Find the limitation that prevents a product-effect or personal-risk conclusion.
4. Identify where the interface should stop, request better information or make uncertainty clearer.

Neutral prompts:

> Where would you look next?
>
> What information is missing?
>
> Does any wording make you more confident than the displayed evidence warrants?

Do not introduce numerical clinical values, thresholds or treatment advice. Record task success, assists and misinterpretations. If the current demo lacks a task's expected field, record the absence as a product issue rather than supplying it verbally.

### 18:00–25:00 · Loria dossier and evidence challenge

Show the current dossier and evidence checklist.

> Before making a product-information or purchasing decision, which three missing documents would you ask for?
>
> Is it clear which statements are Fasolati's own description and which fields have not been verified?
>
> If you encountered a paper about an ingredient or a page for a differently named mouthwash, what would you need before treating it as evidence about Loria?
>
> Which phrase here, if any, could be read as a claim of benefit, availability or approval that the dossier does not support?

Do not supply Gengyve's formulation as Loria's. Do not ask the participant to recommend the rinse, assess efficacy or provide a testimonial.

### 25:00–30:00 · Prioritize and close

> If we fixed one thing first, what should it be—and what misunderstanding would that prevent?
>
> What did we fail to ask?
>
> Is there anything in my notes about your feedback that you would like corrected?

Recap one observation and one proposed change without implying agreement on clinical utility. Explain the agreed note/withdrawal process. Ask whether a single follow-up about the design feedback would be welcome; do not add the person to general marketing.

## Feedback rubric and blank matrix

### Interpretation of ratings

- **0 — Critical misunderstanding:** cannot complete the task or reaches an unsupported product/clinical conclusion.
- **1 — Assisted:** finds the information only after prompting, or the first interpretation needs correction.
- **2 — Unassisted:** completes the task and accurately describes its information boundary.
- **N/O — Not observed:** task not attempted or insufficient observation. Never convert to zero.

Dimensions:

1. **Scope clarity:** distinguishes information, prototype and clinical service.
2. **Provenance finding:** locates source, method/date or explicit missing fields.
3. **Uncertainty recognition:** sees the point at which no conclusion is justified.
4. **Product-evidence separation:** does not transfer ingredient or Gengyve evidence to Loria.
5. **Navigation/accessibility:** can reach details, citations, download and inquiry using their preferred interaction.

Ratings are a qualitative product-review aid, not a validated instrument, clinical performance metric or participant grade.

### Severity and issue triage

- **Blocker:** patient-data exposure path; implied diagnosis/treatment; borrowed formulation; unsupported benefit/access/approval claim.
- **Major:** source, uncertainty or important missing field cannot be found or is consistently misread.
- **Minor:** wording, spacing or navigation friction without changing meaning.

Do not bury a blocker in an average score. Record whether an issue occurred once or repeatedly and retain the denominator of completed tasks. No percentages or general claims about all clinicians from this small convenience sample.

## Gates

### Gate A — Before any recruitment message

- [ ] User approves the program scope, proposed role mix and all public wording.
- [ ] User supplies or approves actual recipients; no candidate list is invented by this kit.
- [ ] Exact recipient, channel, invitation payload and any proposed terms are approved.
- [ ] Materials are versioned; all patient examples are fictional.
- [ ] No unsupported clinical benefit, regulatory status or product-equivalence claim remains.
- [ ] Existing inquiry route handles the `design-partner` intent correctly.
- [ ] No payment, product access or schedule commitment is implied without separate authorization.

**If any item fails: do not recruit.**

### Gate B — Before the first session

- [ ] Moderator, owner and note-handling responsibilities are assigned.
- [ ] Session scope and manual-note permission are agreed.
- [ ] Privacy/governance reviewer approves retention, withdrawal and incident handling.
- [ ] Fictional task works without clinical systems or real data.
- [ ] The moderator has completed one internal dry run.

**If any item fails: do not run the session.**

### Gate C — Before releasing changes informed by the cohort

- [ ] Every blocker has a documented fix and targeted retest.
- [ ] A source/label review confirms no invented Loria detail or Gengyve-to-Loria transfer.
- [ ] Native details, downloads, anchors, themes and inquiry intent pass keyboard and mobile QA.
- [ ] Findings are reported as usability observations, with participant count and limitations—not clinical validation.
- [ ] No names, logos or quotes are used as endorsements.
- [ ] Owner reviews the revision log and explicitly approves release.

Passing a usability gate does not establish safety, efficacy or readiness for clinical use.
